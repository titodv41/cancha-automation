## The useful signal

The parent in the Tampa pickup-group screenshot wants relaxed soccer for ages 5–10, near home, where a beginner can learn without pressure. They can find serious clubs, but cannot easily compare the low-pressure alternatives. This is a concrete discovery problem and a lead for interviews, not proof that recreational programs are absent or that all parents want the same thing.

Cancha's immediate job: make it easy to answer **where can this person play, and what would joining actually involve?** Keep South Florida as the operating focus. The Tampa question can inform the format and a future verified Tampa guide; it is not a reason to switch the whole project's geography again.

## First seven days

1. **Make one parent journey work end to end.** Test a beginner child, recreational soccer and a specific South Florida city. Check count, card facts, detail page, official registration/contact link and mobile back navigation. Keep the selected age audience in the URL. Say when exact program eligibility or prices are unknown.
2. **Add depth in a few useful areas.** Finish the reviewed South Florida camp import, then prioritize local recreational/beginner clubs and programs around Doral/Kendall and Weston/Davie/Pembroke Pines. Each record needs an actual organizer, useful identity media, ages, venue, current season/availability evidence, price when published and a working next action. No quota-filling duplicates or vague claims that a whole directory is verified.
3. **Make the card answer five questions.** Who is it for? Where? What kind of soccer? What is the time/cost commitment? What is the next step? Keep source detail available without repeating warning paragraphs throughout the design. Preserve opportunities, coaches, courses and careers as distinct discoverable categories.
4. **Produce one parent-first content sequence.** Example research briefs: "First soccer season in Weston? Start with rec," "What changes when you move from rec to travel?", and a verified local program comparison. These are proposed topics, not checked listing claims. Use a clear place/age hook, different real media and one next action. Link each post to matching website results, not an unrelated homepage claim.
5. **Add a local person.** The owner records a short coach conversation or obtains a club's answers and cleared media. Ask: who fits, where they train, actual cost, weekly commitment and how a beginner joins. Build the written profile, carousel and Reel from the same verified interview. Do not invent answers or relationships.
6. **Improve discovery from the Instagram profile.** Prepare three useful pinned posts (what Cancha helps with, one strong local guide, how clubs can be listed) and highlight covers for Start here, Parents, Places, Coaches and Clubs. Verify the current bio destination before changing calls to action. Profile/bio changes require account access; API publishing is not proof they were applied.
7. **Review results once a week.** Compare similarly aged, similar-purpose posts. Look at shares/reach, saves/reach, Reel retention when available, relevant website visits, outbound organizer referrals and actual submissions. Report registrations only when an organizer confirms them. Owner/QA visits and clicks are not customers. Keep a short learning note and change one major variable next week.

The existing two-post/day authorization is a ceiling. It does not require publishing a weak second post. Test content on real questions and fresh local stories; do not scale generic templates merely because rendering is automatic. The installed skills do not themselves perform unattended research.

## Cancha for Clubs: the nearest revenue experiment

Start as a section of the existing website, using the same name and trust. Do **not** open a second Instagram feed yet: the founder would have two small audiences and another content obligation. Revisit a separate account when client work, references and a distinct professional audience can support it.

The first offer should be a **defined registration campaign**, not "we do all your marketing":

- One real program and target local audience.
- A clear program/registration page or improvement to the club's existing page.
- A small agreed set of original posts/Reels from club-provided or cleared media.
- A calendar, a relevant enquiry path and a simple referral/reporting summary.
- Club confirmation of dates, ages, prices and media/child permissions before release.

Keep hosting/domain costs and ad spend separate. Do not sell unlimited content, promise registrations or offer pricing before the owner agrees. The club owns its accounts, domain, footage and participant data. Use proper partner/role access, never shared passwords or one client's credentials in another client's workflow.

**Validation:** speak with 5 club directors/coaches, identify one repeated costly problem, then propose 2 tightly scoped paid pilots. The owner handles outreach and approves terms. Measure hours, direct costs, revisions, enquiry quality and whether the club wants to renew. A discounted/free demonstration should not be described as a paid customer.

AI can help with research, drafts, edits, captions, resizing, approved scheduling and reports. It cannot substitute for actual local access, correct program information, account consent, customer service or a human's agreement to an interview. Build a reusable brief → verified assets → review → schedule → receipts → measurement workflow for each client, with separate state and secrets.

Start with the campaign service. Later test a bounded monthly content service, then clearly disclosed featured placement once Cancha can show meaningful audience/referral value. Outreach services require a separate consent-based scope; no scraped-contact mass messages.

Ad-funded discovery needs sustained traffic and introduces another incentive and design constraint. A small service business can test revenue with fewer visitors, but is constrained by delivery time. Track contribution per client and effective hourly return before expanding. The public directory remains useful without paying for ranking; paid placement must be labelled and cannot buy a favorable factual assessment.

## Personalized shin guards: validate later, without inventory

Keep this separate from the immediate Cancha work. Personalized shin guards and products marketed around comfort/protection already exist; for example, [SAK's manufacturer page](https://sakproject.com/shin-guard-models/) advertises personalized designs and [G-Form lists soccer protection products](https://g-form.com/collections/soccer). These manufacturer pages were opened November 2; their marketing is not independent proof of performance. The proposed gap is a hypothesis to test, not a uniqueness claim. Do not promise that a product prevents injury or is league-approved before establishing it.

1. Interview 10 parents and 3 coaches about the guards players actually use: fit, slipping, sweat, comfort, sizing, breakage and what they paid. Ask about real experiences, not just whether the idea sounds nice.
2. Compare existing personalized products, their fit/coverage, materials, prices, return policies and any stated test standards.
3. Shortlist 3 established manufacturers. Ask for samples, size ranges, minimum order, customization process, testing documents, traceability, landed cost, defect/return handling and lead times. Do not place an order or pay without the owner.
4. Test an existing protective design with customization rather than inventing both a safety product and a supply chain at once. Confirm that printing/decoration does not change the tested construction or required marks.
5. Identify the exact youth league/school rules and product requirements before using "approved". There is no single generic youth-organization approval. Products primarily intended for children 12 and younger may be children's products under US rules; applicable testing/certification requirements matter for importers and manufacturers. See the [CPSC guidance](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/childrens-products).
6. Calculate landed unit cost, personalization labor, packaging, shipping, defects, returns and customer-acquisition cost. Compare what remains with the time and risk involved. A low factory quote is not a margin.
7. Use a private interest list or clearly labelled mockup to test personalization/gift demand. No invented testimonials, approval badges or protection claims. Any child's photograph sent for customization requires a private, consented process; it is not public marketing media.

The brand promise can be personal expression and a fit the player likes. Comfort, impact protection, durability and league suitability require evidence. Do not offer "comfortable and strong" as a proven trade-off breakthrough on the basis of interviews alone.
