# Which business model should Cancha test?

Research updated October 21, 2026, after the owner's GoodRec comparison. No payment flow, advertising campaign, subscription or paid placement was activated.

**Recommendation: free discovery now, a small featured-placement experiment next, then one paid local game with a real organizer.** A booking marketplace is a useful direction to test; charging to reveal public contact information is a weak first offer. A native phone app does not solve the underlying demand or payment problem.

## What GoodRec actually sells

GoodRec's player FAQ says people pay in the app to reserve a game spot; prices vary by city, venue, format and time. Its pickup page describes finding the other players, arranging the field/equipment, handling payments and sorting teams. Its partner portal also supports independent organizers/facilities: organizers set the city, venue, time and price, manage players, then receive payouts. The portal says there is no upfront/listing fee and GoodRec takes **a percentage of each booking**. An exact percentage was not stated in the public text reviewed, so do not assume a commission rate.

Its terms distinguish independent organizers and venues from the platform. Therefore Cancha could eventually support organizer-run games; it does not have to own a facility. It would still need actual organizers, confirmed bookable sessions and a reliable service around booking.

Official references: [player FAQ](https://www.goodrec.com/#faq), [pickup soccer](https://www.goodrec.com/pickup-soccer), [organizer/facility partner portal](https://partner.goodrec.tech), [terms](https://www.goodrec.com/terms). The public pages were read; no application or contact request was sent. Their audience and partner-success claims are GoodRec's claims, not Cancha traction or forecasts.

## Compare the models

| Model | What people pay for | Can it be bypassed? | Fit for Cancha now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Display advertising | Advertiser exposure to an audience | No booking attribution needed | Easy to describe, weak economics at current traffic |
| Featured club/coach profiles | Clearly labeled relevant visibility and a report | Contact outside Cancha is the intended outcome | Small manual pilot once relevant visitors can be measured |
| Pay to reveal a phone/email | Access to contact information | Very easily: clubs already have public websites and social pages | Do not build a contact paywall now |
| Pay per qualified enquiry | A defined, legitimate prospective participant | A lead can be measured; later registration needs organizer feedback | Possible later, with an agreed definition and no guaranteed sale |
| Game/training booking fee | A reserved place, checkout, capacity control, confirmations and support | Repeat customers can still book elsewhere | Worth testing with an organizer who values those services |
| Player membership | Repeated useful sessions or benefits | Only valuable if the ongoing service earns renewal | Wait for repeat attendance and reliable supply |

Do not make ordinary club discovery or correction requests paid. A paid listing must not outrank the user's location/age needs or buy a claim of better coaching.

## Why people might keep booking through Cancha

No software can guarantee that two people will never arrange their next session directly. The durable approach is to provide something beyond an introduction: confirmed capacity, an actual game, easy payment, a visible cancellation policy, reminders, waitlist replacements and a usable attendance record. An organizer agreement can define how bookings and commission work, but it cannot replace value. Avoid trying to hide public contacts or punish players for talking to each other.

Measure a booking from a successful payment-provider event, not a redirect to a club's site. Match the payment to an event ID, player reservation and organizer; confirm attendance privately and record cancellations/refunds. A click is a referral, not a paid booking. For an enquiry pilot, count unique consenting valid submissions and let the organizer report whether they converted. Do not claim off-platform sales you cannot observe.

## Illustrative economics, not a forecast

**Display ads:** at an *assumed* $5 page RPM, 10,000 monetized pageviews/month would be $50, 100,000 would be $500 and one million would be $5,000, before other costs. The RPM and fill are unknown. Early owner/QA traffic is not a sellable audience. A single relevant local sponsor could be more useful than programmatic ads at low volume, but demand still has to be tested.

**A game Cancha organizes:** 20 paid spots × an *assumed* $12 = $240 gross. With an *assumed* $100 field booking, $40 host cost and Stripe's listed US domestic card rate of 2.9% + $0.30 on each payment, payment fees would be about $12.96. Contribution would be about **$87.04**, before other costs, refunds, taxes and founder time. Financial break-even on those assumptions is 13 paid spots; the actual game may need a different minimum to run well. None of these prices or venue costs has been quoted or agreed.

**A partner game:** if an organizer sells the same $240 of spots and Cancha hypothetically takes 10%, the platform earns $24 gross, not the whole $240. If Cancha also pays the $12.96 card fees, only $11.04 remains before support/other costs. Decide who pays processing fees and how refunds work before agreeing a commission. The 10% is an arithmetic example, **not GoodRec's fee or a proposed live Cancha rate**.

The distinction matters: an organized-game margin and a marketplace commission are different businesses. [Stripe official pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing) is the rate reference; country/payment method/platform payout products can add different fees.

## A realistic sequence for a solo founder

1. Keep improving free discovery and gather genuine club/player feedback. Use the existing private submission form and tagged links. Do not build accounts solely to gate browsing.
2. Test up to three disclosed featured profiles in one area with an honest exposure report. The [featured-placement pilot](monetization-pilot.md) defines free trials, draft pricing tests and measurement.
3. Validate one adult pickup session with a willing organizer, confirmed venue permission, host, time, player capacity and real costs. First collect interest with consent; an interest group is not a confirmed game.
4. Only after those details are agreed, prepare one hosted checkout/payment link and manual check-in/cancellation process. No new subscription platform is necessary for the experiment. Do not collect payment for an unconfirmed session. Publishing a payment link and incurring venue costs require the owner's concrete approval at that stage.
5. Review paid attendance, repeat intent, cancellations, player acquisition cost, contribution per game and founder time. Repeat a few sessions before building automated payouts, memberships or a native app.

A responsive web experience and shareable booking links are enough to test the proposition. Consider a native app when repeat usage reveals a specific need—such as repeat bookings or notifications—that justifies its extra development and support.

## Media and trust on the homepage

Club badges are identifying directory content, not sponsors or endorsements. The new badge carousel links to real profiles and says **Clubs in the directory**. The homepage's short **How Cancha works** section describes today's actual process: search, compare, contact the organizer. It does not pretend checkout is already available.

The current hero uses a real 2024 photograph of Orlando supporters. Source, creator, license and context are available in the footer’s Photo credits section. These are illustrative photographs, not Cancha-organized events or endorsements. On phones, search comes first and a full-width photograph replaces the cramped portrait crop. The club badges move gently, with pause controls and reduced-motion support. Future organizer-supplied photos and real community clips will give Cancha a stronger visual identity than stock sports shots. Obtain permission before presenting people or clubs as supporters; paid ad permission is separate from identifying a directory listing.
